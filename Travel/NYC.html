Learn the grid and put your phone away

Spend a few minutes understanding the Manhattan grid, because it's a great navigation system built right into the city.

Streets run east-west and count upward as you go north: 42nd Street, 43rd, 44th. Avenues run north-south and count upward as you go west: First Avenue at the East River, up through Fifth in the middle, to Eleventh near the Hudson. Fifth Avenue is the dividing line: addresses east of it are "East 46th," west of it are "West 46th."

Once you have the X-Y coordinates in your head, any corner tells you exactly where you are and which way to walk. Now you can look around at the city instead of looking at your map.

This becomes even more important on a Citi Bike. The bikes are fun, and you don't want to be holding your phone in one hand while riding in traffic. Also make sure to install the Citi Bike app and understand the rules: the meter starts charging by the minute once you hit the 30-minute mark. Docking spots are everywhere, so just dock and re-undock before the half hour to reset the counter.

The one place the grid can't help you is underground. For the subway, Google Maps is your friend. Riding the subway is easier than in Japan! Tap your phone or credit card at the turnstile, ride as far as you want, and no tap out is required.

Grand Central Terminal

Walk into the Main Concourse, stop in the middle of the floor, and look up: a green vault painted with the constellations of the zodiac, gold-leafed stars, twenty-five hundred of them. The whole sky, rendered backwards. Famously, the painters worked from a medieval manuscript that showed the heavens from God's perspective, looking down.

Grand Central Madison

Beside the old terminal is Grand Central Madison, the LIRR station that opened in 2023. Enter around 43rd Street and walk the full concourse underground to 48th Street. The concourse and terminals are lined with art installations: enormous glass mosaic murals by Yayoi Kusama and Kiki Smith, and digital displays. Take the very long escalator down into the station itself, dubbed the meditation ride, and it would be one, too, if not for the loud safety announcements.

Le Parisien Bakery - 235 W 46th St

This is as close as you get to Paris without going to Paris. Cappuccino, omelette, ham and cheese croissant.

MoMA - 11 W 53rd St

Vincent van Gogh, The Starry Night. Smaller but more turbulent and 3D than expected.
Claude Monet, Water Lilies triptych. Massive, immersive, multi-panel murals of his water garden, wrapping around a dedicated gallery. Sit on the bench in the middle of the room. Better Monet than Met.
Pablo Picasso. Huge canvases, unfinished edges, the confidence to leave things raw. Decades long evolution.
Salvador Dalí, The Persistence of Memory. Tiny with a shiny, almost metallic finish and surprisingly extreme detail.
Andy Warhol, Campbell's Soup Cans. Thirty-two canvases, one per flavor, hung in a grid. Hand-made to look mechanical.

Biking Central Park

Grab a Citi Bike near the southern edge of the park and ride the loop. Ride counterclockwise, everyone does that, past the Sheep Meadow, Bethesda Terrace, the Reservoir. Dock the bike near the east side around 82nd Street to go to the Met.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art

The Temple of Dendur. A real Egyptian temple, two thousand years old, reassembled in a glass-walled hall with a reflecting pool.
The Roof Garden. Head up via the elevator in the Medieval wing. Panoramic views of the skyline over the treetops of Central Park, plus a seasonal art installation.
The Astor Chinese Garden Court. A Ming-dynasty-style scholar's courtyard built by craftsmen from Suzhou using traditional joinery - no nails. Absolute stillness.
Van Gogh's masterworks. Wheat fields, cypresses, self-portraits, sunflowers. Continued from MoMA.

Walk out onto the Fifth Avenue steps, sit for a minute like everyone else.

Russ & Daughters - 502 W 34th St

The lox bagel - the best lox bagel in the world. Don't be annoying and order online before walking in.

The Vessel

Climb the Vessel right next to Russ & Daughters.

The High Line

Walk onto the High Line right next to the Vessel. This is an elevated freight rail line turned linear park - wildflower plantings threaded through the old tracks, art installations, the city sliding past at third-story height. Walk south toward Little Island, dropping down at 16th Street for Chelsea Market.

Chelsea Market

Los Tacos No. 1, as close as you can get to San Diego without going to San Diego. The hot sauce is hot!
Miznon, the taste of Tel Aviv in NYC. How do you roast the whole cauliflower?

Little Island

The park that floats above the Hudson on tulip-shaped concrete columns. Wander up its little hills and depending on the time, you might be able to catch a show in the amphitheater.

IAC building

Pick up a Citi Bike, bike north to Pier 59, and take a U-turn. Across 11th avenue (looking east) is the wall of digital art visible from the bike trail - a massive screen of shifting generative work through the glass wall of the IAC building.

Greenway south to Battery Park

Keep riding, or walk on the stretches where you feel like it, the river on your right, the city on your left, the new World Trade Center rising ahead. Ride it all the way to the Battery, then loop back north a bit and dock.

Battery Park City Esplanade and North Cove

Walk the Battery Park City Esplanade - landscaped gardens, benches facing the harbor, the Statue of Liberty out in the water. It curves into North Cove Yacht Harbor, a small marina full of improbably large sailboats parked in the shadow of the Financial District.

Brookfield Place

Step inside Brookfield Place to marvel at all that polished marble along with the Winter Garden's glass vault and its indoor grove of palm trees. A cathedral of commerce, and blissfully air-conditioned.

The 9/11 Memorial

Walk the two memorial pools. The water falls into voids the exact footprints of the towers, and the sound of it swallows the city.

The Oculus

Then go deep into the Oculus, down into the bowels of the giant bird, exposed ribs making it feel like you are inside something alive, level after level of white marble, the deeper you go the more overwhelming the whiteness gets.

PATH to Exchange Place - the view back

From inside the Oculus, take the PATH train one stop under the river to Exchange Place in Jersey City. Walk out onto the Hudson River Waterfront Walkway. The entire lower Manhattan skyline is in one view across the water - everything you just did: the greenway you biked, the esplanade you walked, the towers you stood beneath. Walk the waterfront as far as you want, then PATH back.

Xi'an Famous Foods - Chinatown

Head down to Chinatown for Xi'an Famous Foods for hand-pulled biang biang noodles - wide, chewy ribbons slicked with chili oil and cumin lamb, torn by hand to order. Or wander the neighborhood's other famous spots.

The Brooklyn Bridge - walk it, don't bike it

The pedestrian promenade runs along the upper level, elevated above the roadway on the bridge's center line, while the bike lane sits down at car level. The walkway is higher than the bike path with higher views, wooden planks underfoot, the Gothic granite towers and the web of cables overhead. Walk it all the way to Brooklyn, turn around, and walk back: the Manhattan skyline growing in front of you the entire way until you walk right back into it.
